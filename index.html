Bipin Lekhak

PhD candidate, Computing and Information Sciences
Rochester Institute of Technology · Computational Biomedicine Laboratory

I work on causal machine learning for time series. I build latent-variable models that learn a system's dynamics well enough to predict how it will respond to interventions: a tumour under chemo- or radiotherapy, a patient in intensive care, a physical system observed only through video. My models separate a system's native dynamics from its response to treatment, are identifiable, and adapt to each individual from its own history through meta-learning.

I am advised by Prof. Linwei Wang. Before the PhD I spent four years as a machine learning engineer at Fusemachines in Kathmandu, building medical image segmentation, 3D shape reconstruction and forecasting systems.

I am looking for postdoctoral fellowships and research positions starting in 2027 in causal inference, time-series modelling and machine learning for health.

Research

How will a system respond to an intervention it has not yet received? I build models that answer this from observational time series, and the guarantees needed to trust their answers.

Interventional latent dynamics

Forecasting a patient's trajectory under a candidate treatment plan needs two things at once: the patient's own disease dynamics, and how those dynamics change under treatment. ASIDE and its successor iASIDE model these as separate components of a latent ordinary differential equation. Meta-learned encoders read a subject's history and infer the parameters of each component, so the model adapts to every individual without retraining. iASIDE shows that the latent interventional dynamics are identifiable, and gives a separable formulation that reaches the identifiable solution in practice.

Evaluated on tumour growth under chemo- and radiotherapy, MIMIC-III intensive-care data and videos of forced pendulums, with and without time-varying confounding, against established baselines such as the Causal Transformer, CRN and RMSN.

  • Neural ODEs
  • Meta-learning
  • Identifiability
  • Variational inference
  • Time-varying confounding

Treatment effects over time

Separating native from response dynamics gives a natural handle on causal effects. With collaborators I am developing temporal versions of meta-learners, the X- and R-learners, that use this structure to estimate how outcomes would differ under alternative treatment sequences. This work also corrects for treatment-assignment bias with weighting and invariant representations.

  • Potential outcomes
  • Meta-learners
  • Inverse probability weighting

Remote monitoring after hospital discharge

Many heart-failure patients return to hospital within weeks of discharge. In an NIH-funded study with the University of Rochester Medical Center, I analysed data from a sensor-equipped toilet seat that recorded cardiac measurements at home for 90 days after discharge. We measured how many patients could be enrolled and how consistently they used the device, documented why in-home deployments failed, and showed that the seat's measurements predict hospitalisation within 21 days (AUC 0.74).

  • Care after discharge
  • Heart failure
  • Clinical risk prediction
